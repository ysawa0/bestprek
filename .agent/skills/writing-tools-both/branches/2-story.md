# Layer 2: Story or hybrid

Read at Layer 2 only when this branch applies.

### 29. Foreshadow dramatic events and powerful conclusions.

Requirement: In a story, introduce the detail or question that prepares the major turn before the turn occurs. Bring it back at the payoff. Distinguish evidence available at the time from hindsight; use only sourced clues in nonfiction.

Pass: Each major narrative payoff has an earlier setup; setup and payoff match the actual record.

### 30. To generate suspense, use internal cliffhangers.

Requirement: At an internal story-section break, end on an unresolved consequence, obstacle, or question. Resolve it in the subsequent narrative. Keep the promised stakes equal to the actual payoff. In reports and instructions, give the needed answer immediately instead.

Pass: Every internal narrative cliffhanger has a later payoff; no operational answer is delayed for suspense.

### 35. Report and write for scenes.

Requirement: Build each scene from time, place, participants, action, and a resulting change. Use scenes for pivotal events and summary for intervening time. If the nonfiction source lacks scene-level evidence, retain factual summary and record the missing evidence.

Pass: Every scene has all five elements and sourced nonfiction details; missing observations remain gaps, not inventions.

### 36. Mix narrative modes.

Requirement: Use scene for a pivotal event, summary to cross uneventful time, and exposition to supply a necessary explanation. Put exposition at the point its information is needed. Mark each change of time, place, or mode.

Pass: Each passage's mode matches its job; transitions preserve chronology and orientation.
