# Blueprints · Requirements 24–39

## 24. Work from a plan.

Requirement: Before drafting, write a working outline that states the opening, the job of each section, and the ending. For a short message, use one line. For revision, map the existing structure before moving material. Make the final sequence match the plan or update the plan to match the corrected sequence.

Pass: Every section has a defined job and position; the whole has an opening-to-ending route.

## 25. Learn the difference between reports and stories.

Requirement: Classify the piece before drafting. Report: give the answer or current status first, then evidence and action. Story: show a disruption, consequential action, and change. Hybrid: give the report's answer first, then a clearly bounded narrative. Apply narrative rules within story passages only.

Pass: The opening and sequence follow the declared form; operational answers are not withheld to manufacture suspense.

## 26. Use dialogue as a form of action.

Requirement: Make every exchange change a decision, relationship, obstacle, or the reader's knowledge. Cut dialogue that repeats narration without advancing the scene. In nonfiction, use documented wording inside quotation marks and summarize undocumented exchanges without quotation marks.

Pass: Every exchange changes something; every nonfiction quotation is traceable to its source.

## 27. Reveal traits of character.

Requirement: Establish a character trait through an observable choice, action, habit, or sacrifice before naming the trait. Keep judgments about motive separate from reported behavior. Use more than a trait label to distinguish the person.

Pass: Each stated trait has behavioral evidence; inferred motives are identified as inferences rather than reported facts.

## 28. Put odd and interesting things next to each other.

Requirement: Place the two sides of a contrast in adjacent sentences or paragraphs. Identify the shared comparison axis and state the revealing difference. Preserve mismatched baselines and uncertainty explicitly instead of implying equivalence.

Pass: The contrasting facts are adjacent, comparable on a named basis, and not presented as an unsupported causal relationship.

## 29. Foreshadow dramatic events and powerful conclusions.

Requirement: In a story, introduce the detail or question that prepares the major turn before the turn occurs. Bring it back at the payoff. Distinguish evidence available at the time from hindsight; use only sourced clues in nonfiction.

Pass: Each major narrative payoff has an earlier setup; setup and payoff match the actual record.

## 30. To generate suspense, use internal cliffhangers.

Requirement: At an internal story-section break, end on an unresolved consequence, obstacle, or question. Resolve it in the subsequent narrative. Keep the promised stakes equal to the actual payoff. In reports and instructions, give the needed answer immediately instead.

Pass: Every internal narrative cliffhanger has a later payoff; no operational answer is delayed for suspense.

## 31. Build your work around a key question.

Requirement: Write the central question in a working note before drafting. Make each section answer it, supply necessary evidence, or resolve a supporting question. Remove material with none of those jobs. For a reference document, use the reader's common lookup need as the question.

Pass: Every section has a direct relationship to the central question; the ending answers it or states the evidence gap.

## 32. Place gold coins along the path.

Requirement: Give every substantive section a concrete reader payoff: a revealing fact, useful example, resolved question, actionable instruction, or new implication. Distribute those payoffs through the piece instead of saving all value for the ending.

Pass: Every substantive section delivers identifiable new value; none consists solely of setup or repetition.

## 33. Repeat, repeat, and repeat.

Requirement: Repeat the central term at the opening, a major transition, and the conclusion of a multi-section piece. Give the recurrence a new job each time: establish, develop, resolve. In shorter pieces, repeat only for reference or an explicit rhetorical pattern. Keep wording stable for the same concept.

Pass: Long-form recurrence follows establish–develop–resolve; every other repetition has a reference or pattern function.

## 34. Write from different cinematic angles.

Requirement: In scene-based writing, move through three scales: orient the setting, show the participant's action, then isolate a telling detail. Signal changes of place or viewpoint. Build every nonfiction scale from documented observation.

Pass: Scenes contain setting, action, and detail; the reader can identify the viewpoint and any change of scale.

## 35. Report and write for scenes.

Requirement: Build each scene from time, place, participants, action, and a resulting change. Use scenes for pivotal events and summary for intervening time. If the nonfiction source lacks scene-level evidence, retain factual summary and record the missing evidence.

Pass: Every scene has all five elements and sourced nonfiction details; missing observations remain gaps, not inventions.

## 36. Mix narrative modes.

Requirement: Use scene for a pivotal event, summary to cross uneventful time, and exposition to supply a necessary explanation. Put exposition at the point its information is needed. Mark each change of time, place, or mode.

Pass: Each passage's mode matches its job; transitions preserve chronology and orientation.

## 37. In short works, don't waste a syllable.

Requirement: For a short-form brief, remove preambles, repeated context, redundant modifiers, and closing restatements. Give each word an informational, grammatical, rhythmic, or voice-preserving job. Meet the requested length without sacrificing necessary qualifications or unique facts.

Pass: The text meets its length limit, preserves the required meaning, and contains no removable setup or repetition.

## 38. Prefer archetypes to stereotypes.

Requirement: Ground a recognizable role in the individual's actions, circumstances, and contradictions. Replace stock trait bundles with the person's specific behavior. Use demographic facts only as facts; establish personality and motive from separate evidence.

Pass: The individual is distinguishable from the role; no trait or motive rests solely on demographic membership.

## 39. Write toward an ending.

Requirement: Choose the final answer, decision, action, resolution, or image before polishing. Make the preceding material prepare it. End immediately after delivering it. Place essential qualifications before the final emphasis and preserve their scope.

Pass: The ending completes the opening promise, introduces no unsupported claim, and has no trailing generic recap.
