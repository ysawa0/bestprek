# Bonus tools

Always rule 53 lives in SKILL.md.

## 51. Take advantage of narrative numbers.

Requirement: Attach units and timeframes to quantities. Give changes their baseline and comparison period. Distinguish counts, rates, percent changes, and percentage-point changes. Label estimates, extrapolations, and realized results. Verify each derived calculation and pair consequential figures with a concrete comparison from the evidence.

Pass: Every number has interpretable scope; comparisons and arithmetic are correct; projections remain labeled as projections.

## 52. Express your best thought in the shortest sentence.

Requirement: State the central insight in a standalone sentence. Make it the shortest complete authored sentence in the piece; exact source quotations are outside that comparison. Move supporting explanation around it while preserving any qualification that determines its truth. Apply Rule 2 to its word order.

Pass: The central insight occupies the shortest complete authored sentence and remains accurate on its own.

## 54. Create a mosaic of detail to reveal character.

Requirement: For a character portrait, combine details from different categories: action, speech, habit, possessions, relationships, or a contradiction. Make each detail add a different facet. Replace multiple examples of the same trait with complementary evidence.

Pass: The portrait contains more than one facet; every nonfiction detail is sourced and no inferred motive is presented as observation.

## 55. Look for the “inciting incident” to kick-start your story.

Requirement: Start a story with the event that disrupts the prior situation and triggers the central action. Put necessary backstory after that event. In a hybrid, place the story's trigger immediately after the report lead. Preserve the actual event, stakes, and sequence in nonfiction.

Pass: The narrative opens on a specific disruption with a consequence; backstory does not delay the trigger.
