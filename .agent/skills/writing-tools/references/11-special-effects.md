# Special effects · Requirements 11–23

## 11. Prefer the simple over the technical.

Requirement: Use the simplest word that preserves the exact meaning. Replace jargon with its plain equivalent. Retain a specialist term when it names a distinction the plain replacement loses; define it on first use for a nonexpert audience. Keep code identifiers exact.

Pass: Each specialist term is necessary for precision or is an exact identifier; unfamiliar necessary terms are defined for the reader.

## 12. Give key words their space.

Requirement: Use a distinctive emphatic word once in its local passage; reserve its next occurrence for deliberate recurrence under Rule 33. Remove accidental nearby echoes. Use the same technical name for the same thing throughout; do not create false distinctions through synonym swapping.

Pass: Repeated emphatic words have an identifiable structural role; repeated entity names remain consistent.

## 13. Play with words, even in serious stories.

Requirement: In playful prose, build wordplay from the subject's actual vocabulary and make its literal meaning intelligible. In serious prose, create verbal play through parallelism, sound, or a precise contrast rather than a joke at the subject's expense. Keep condolence, emergency, and strictly factual status text literal.

Pass: Expressive prose has a subject-based verbal pattern or turn; protected literal forms contain no inserted joke.

## 14. Get the name of the dog.

Requirement: Replace a generic description of a central person, place, object, or event with a concrete identifying detail from the source. Use the actual name, number, model, action, or observable feature. Record a missing detail as an evidence gap rather than filling it with invention.

Pass: Central descriptions contain sourced specifics; every added nonfiction detail is supported or explicitly marked as missing.

## 15. Pay attention to names.

Requirement: Check every name, title, label, and spelling against the source. Introduce people and components unambiguously, then name them consistently. Apply requested anonymization consistently. Give fictional characters distinct names and keep the name-to-character mapping stable.

Pass: Each name identifies one referent, matches its source or fictional assignment, and follows the chosen naming convention.

## 16. Seek original images.

Requirement: Replace a stock metaphor with a concrete observation or a new comparison derived from the subject. For each comparison, identify the exact shared property. Keep one coherent image instead of mixing incompatible comparisons.

Pass: Every image supplies a specific correspondence; no stock or mixed metaphor survives.

## 17. Riff on the creative language of others.

Requirement: When borrowing language, distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and allusion. Quote exactly and credit the source; restate paraphrases in original wording and attribute distinctive borrowed ideas. Build the new passage around the present subject rather than copying another writer's expression.

Pass: Borrowed language has a clear source and form; new language does new work rather than masquerading as an original quotation.

## 18. Set the pace with sentence length.

Requirement: Mark changes of direction with short independent sentences. Carry a continuous sequence in connected clauses. Change sentence length at an argumentative or narrative turn instead of repeating one sentence shape throughout.

Pass: The cadence changes at each turn; continuous action and decisive stops have different sentence shapes.

## 19. Vary the lengths of paragraphs.

Requirement: Give each paragraph one topic or narrative beat. Start a new paragraph when that job changes. Put a pivotal takeaway in a one-sentence paragraph and develop its evidence in surrounding paragraphs. In a one-paragraph format, preserve the format and mark the turn within it.

Pass: Paragraph breaks follow changes of job; the pivotal point is visibly distinguished without violating the requested format.

## 20. Choose the number of elements with a purpose in mind.

Requirement: Build series by function: one for focus, two for contrast, three for a completed pattern, four or more for inventory or accumulation. Give every item a distinct role. Retain every item the user requires; remove filler added only to reach a count.

Pass: The series count corresponds to its function; no duplicate or invented item exists solely to make a pattern.

## 21. Know when to back off and when to show off.

Requirement: Describe harm, grief, danger, and major factual results with concrete nouns and direct verbs. Remove evaluative intensifiers and decorative commentary from those passages. In explanatory or playful passages, make each flourish carry a comparison, distinction, or insight.

Pass: High-stakes passages let the facts carry the weight; every flourish elsewhere adds identifiable meaning.

## 22. Climb up and down the ladder of abstraction.

Requirement: Pair each central abstract claim with a concrete example, observation, or measurement. Explain what the example establishes and where that inference stops. Connect concrete detail back to the general point instead of leaving either level unsupported.

Pass: The reader can move from claim to example and back; the inference does not exceed the evidence.

## 23. Tune your voice.

Requirement: Set person, register, attitude, and distance from the brief or supplied draft. Keep them consistent throughout. Preserve the author's deliberate conversational expressions while correcting the prose around them. Read the finished text for shifts into an unrequested register.

Pass: The voice matches the brief from opening to ending; edits have not replaced the author's voice with generic formal prose.
