# Layer 1: Plan

Run before any writing.

Apply the Always section and each rule whose target appears.

Done when every applied rule here passes its Pass line.

## Always

### 25. Learn the difference between reports and stories.

Requirement: Classify the piece before drafting. Report: give the answer or current status first, then evidence and action. Story: show a disruption, consequential action, and change. Hybrid: give the report's answer first, then a clearly bounded narrative. Apply narrative rules within story passages only.

Pass: The opening and sequence follow the declared form; operational answers are not withheld to manufacture suspense.

### 40. Draft a mission statement for your work.

Requirement: Before drafting or revising, write one working sentence: “For [reader], this piece will [purpose] so they can [result].” Use it to decide what belongs. Keep the mission out of the deliverable unless requested.

Pass: The working mission identifies reader, purpose, and result; the finished piece fulfills that same mission.

### 24. Work from a plan.

Requirement: Before drafting, write a working outline that states the opening, the job of each section, and the ending. For a short message, use one line. For revision, map the existing structure before moving material. Make the final sequence match the plan or update the plan to match the corrected sequence.

Pass: Every section has a defined job and position; the whole has an opening-to-ending route.

## When the target appears

### 31. Build your work around a key question.

Target: A multi-section piece.

Requirement: Write the central question in a working note before drafting. Make each section answer it, supply necessary evidence, or resolve a supporting question. Remove material with none of those jobs. For a reference document, use the reader's common lookup need as the question.

Pass: Every section has a direct relationship to the central question; the ending answers it or states the evidence gap.

### 42. Do your homework well in advance.

Target: New factual material or added claims.

Requirement: Before writing new factual material, identify and obtain the evidence for its key claims. Record source locations and unresolved gaps. For a supplied-text rewrite, use the supplied facts; verify any added claim before including it. Finish evidence collection before polishing factual claims.

Pass: Every added factual claim has a source; every unresolved dependency is marked rather than fabricated.

### 45. Break long projects into parts.

Target: A multi-section piece.

Requirement: Divide a multi-section project into units with a defined job, inputs, and completion condition. Draft each against the shared brief and terminology. Reassemble the units and remove duplicate claims, missing links, and inconsistent terminology.

Pass: Every unit has a completion condition; the assembled piece is connected and internally consistent.

### 48. Limit self-criticism in early drafts.

Target: Drafting.

Requirement: Complete a beginning-to-ending draft before line editing. Mark uncertainty in working notes and continue. After the complete draft exists, run the structural and sentence-level passes. Keep generating and judging as separate phases.

Pass: A complete draft precedes polishing; all temporary factual gaps are resolved or disclosed before delivery.
