# Writing Tools

The single entry point for prose work. Match the task to a playbook, then apply the rules the index below triggers. Rule blocks live in `references/`, so read the block in full for every rule you apply.

## Non-negotiables

- Lock the evidence. Preserve facts, quantities, units, dates, names, attribution, quotations, uncertainty, causality, and ownership. Keep measurements distinct from projections. Verify an added factual claim or mark the gap. Invent scene details only in fiction.
- Respect the scope. Edit only what the user authorized. A spelling-only request stays spelling-only. Leave exact quotations and out-of-scope text unchanged.
- Keep the genre. Classify the piece as report, story, or hybrid (Rule 25). A rule with no target in the text has no violation. Never add dialogue, characters, metaphors, or suspense just to activate a rule.
- Rules are mandatory. An applied rule is a requirement, not a suggestion. Rule 2's 2–3–1 order holds in every sentence under editorial control.
- Stay quiet. Return the requested prose with only essential gap or conflict notes. Keep rule numbers and checks out of the output unless the user asked for an audit.
- Stop at pass. Stop polishing once every applied rule passes and the requested format and length are met. Disclose an irreconcilable conflict rather than claiming a pass.

## Playbooks

Open a todo list whose first items are the matched playbook's steps, copied verbatim from `playbooks/<name>.md`.

- Draft. New prose from a brief, notes, or facts. File: [draft](playbooks/draft.md).
- Revise. Edit, tighten, shorten, or restyle supplied prose. File: [revise](playbooks/revise.md).
- Audit. Check prose against named rules or all 55, without rewriting unless asked. File: [audit](playbooks/audit.md).

## Rules

Each entry names when it applies. Rules marked always apply to every draft and revision. A triggered rule applies when its target appears or the playbook names it. An exhaustive or all-55 request applies every rule.

[Nuts and bolts](references/01-nuts-and-bolts.md)

1. Begin sentences with subjects and verbs. When: always.
2. Order words for emphasis. When: always. 2–3–1 in every sentence.
3. Activate your verbs. When: always.
4. Be passive-aggressive. When: always.
5. Watch those adverbs. When: always.
6. Take it easy on the -ings. When: progressive or participial constructions appear.
7. Fear not the long sentence. When: a long sentence appears.
8. Establish a pattern, then give it a twist. When: a series or setup-and-turn sequence appears.
9. Let punctuation control pace and space. When: always.
10. Cut big, then small. When: always.

[Special effects](references/11-special-effects.md)

11. Prefer the simple over the technical. When: always.
12. Give key words their space. When: a key word or technical name recurs.
13. Play with words, even in serious stories. When: wordplay appears or the brief invites it.
14. Get the name of the dog. When: a central person, place, object, or event is described generically.
15. Pay attention to names. When: names, titles, or labels appear.
16. Seek original images. When: a metaphor or comparison appears.
17. Riff on the creative language of others. When: quotation, paraphrase, or allusion appears.
18. Set the pace with sentence length. When: always.
19. Vary the lengths of paragraphs. When: the piece has more than one paragraph or a pivotal takeaway.
20. Choose the number of elements with a purpose in mind. When: a series or list appears.
21. Know when to back off and when to show off. When: harm, grief, danger, major results, or flourishes appear.
22. Climb up and down the ladder of abstraction. When: a central abstract claim appears.
23. Tune your voice. When: always.

49. Learn from your critics. When: critique or reviewer feedback is supplied.
