# Story

Applies to pieces tagged story, and to the narrative part of a hybrid.

### 13. Play with words, even in serious stories.

Requirement: In playful prose, build wordplay from the subject's actual vocabulary and make its literal meaning intelligible. In serious prose, create verbal play through parallelism, sound, or a precise contrast rather than a joke at the subject's expense. Keep condolence, emergency, and strictly factual status text literal.

Pass: Expressive prose has a subject-based verbal pattern or turn; protected literal forms contain no inserted joke.

### 26. Use dialogue as a form of action.

Requirement: Make every exchange change a decision, relationship, obstacle, or the reader's knowledge. Cut dialogue that repeats narration without advancing the scene. In nonfiction, use documented wording inside quotation marks and summarize undocumented exchanges without quotation marks.

Pass: Every exchange changes something; every nonfiction quotation is traceable to its source.

### 27. Reveal traits of character.

Requirement: Establish a character trait through an observable choice, action, habit, or sacrifice before naming the trait. Keep judgments about motive separate from reported behavior. Use more than a trait label to distinguish the person.

Pass: Each stated trait has behavioral evidence; inferred motives are identified as inferences rather than reported facts.

### 29. Foreshadow dramatic events and powerful conclusions.

Requirement: In a story, introduce the detail or question that prepares the major turn before the turn occurs. Bring it back at the payoff. Distinguish evidence available at the time from hindsight; use only sourced clues in nonfiction.

Pass: Each major narrative payoff has an earlier setup; setup and payoff match the actual record.

### 30. To generate suspense, use internal cliffhangers.

Requirement: At an internal story-section break, end on an unresolved consequence, obstacle, or question. Resolve it in the subsequent narrative. Keep the promised stakes equal to the actual payoff. In reports and instructions, give the needed answer immediately instead.

Pass: Every internal narrative cliffhanger has a later payoff; no operational answer is delayed for suspense.

### 34. Write from different cinematic angles.

Requirement: In scene-based writing, move through three scales: orient the setting, show the participant's action, then isolate a telling detail. Signal changes of place or viewpoint. Build every nonfiction scale from documented observation.

Pass: Scenes contain setting, action, and detail; the reader can identify the viewpoint and any change of scale.

### 35. Report and write for scenes.

Requirement: Build each scene from time, place, participants, action, and a resulting change. Use scenes for pivotal events and summary for intervening time. If the nonfiction source lacks scene-level evidence, retain factual summary and record the missing evidence.

Pass: Every scene has all five elements and sourced nonfiction details; missing observations remain gaps, not inventions.

### 36. Mix narrative modes.

Requirement: Use scene for a pivotal event, summary to cross uneventful time, and exposition to supply a necessary explanation. Put exposition at the point its information is needed. Mark each change of time, place, or mode.

Pass: Each passage's mode matches its job; transitions preserve chronology and orientation.

### 38. Prefer archetypes to stereotypes.

Requirement: Ground a recognizable role in the individual's actions, circumstances, and contradictions. Replace stock trait bundles with the person's specific behavior. Use demographic facts only as facts; establish personality and motive from separate evidence.

Pass: The individual is distinguishable from the role; no trait or motive rests solely on demographic membership.

### 54. Create a mosaic of detail to reveal character.

Requirement: For a character portrait, combine details from different categories: action, speech, habit, possessions, relationships, or a contradiction. Make each detail add a different facet. Replace multiple examples of the same trait with complementary evidence.

Pass: The portrait contains more than one facet; every nonfiction detail is sourced and no inferred motive is presented as observation.

### 55. Look for the “inciting incident” to kick-start your story.

Requirement: Start a story with the event that disrupts the prior situation and triggers the central action. Put necessary backstory after that event. In a hybrid, place the story's trigger immediately after the report lead. Preserve the actual event, stakes, and sequence in nonfiction.

Pass: The narrative opens on a specific disruption with a consequence; backstory does not delay the trigger.
