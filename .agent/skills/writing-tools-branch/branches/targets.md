# Targets

Each rule applies once its target appears in the piece.

### 6. Take it easy on the -ings.

Target: Progressive or participial constructions.

Requirement: Express completed actions with finite verbs. Retain a progressive construction only for action genuinely in progress, and retain a participial construction only when its subject and timing are explicit. Replace chains of -ing actions with finite clauses in the actual sequence.

Pass: Every progressive encodes ongoing action; every participle has the correct subject; no -ing chain conceals chronology.

### 7. Fear not the long sentence.

Target: A long sentence.

Requirement: Give every long sentence one main-clause spine; attach its details in a traceable order. Keep connected material in that sentence rather than splitting it solely because of word count. Split at a change of actor, time, or logic that otherwise obscures the relationship.

Pass: The main clause and each attachment are identifiable on one reading; sentence length alone has not determined a cut.

### 8. Establish a pattern, then give it a twist.

Target: A series or setup-and-turn sequence.

Requirement: Use parallel grammatical forms for equivalent items. In a setup-and-turn sequence, establish the repeated form before the turn, then place the pattern-breaking item last. Make that item carry the change in meaning.

Pass: Equivalent items are parallel; a deliberate break occurs at the turn rather than by accident.

### 12. Give key words their space.

Target: A recurring key word or technical name.

Requirement: Use a distinctive emphatic word once in its local passage; reserve its next occurrence for deliberate recurrence under Rule 33. Remove accidental nearby echoes. Use the same technical name for the same thing throughout; do not create false distinctions through synonym swapping.

Pass: Repeated emphatic words have an identifiable structural role; repeated entity names remain consistent.

### 14. Get the name of the dog.

Target: A central person, place, object, or event described generically.

Requirement: Replace a generic description of a central person, place, object, or event with a concrete identifying detail from the source. Use the actual name, number, model, action, or observable feature. Record a missing detail as an evidence gap rather than filling it with invention.

Pass: Central descriptions contain sourced specifics; every added nonfiction detail is supported or explicitly marked as missing.

### 15. Pay attention to names.

Target: Names, titles, or labels.

Requirement: Check every name, title, label, and spelling against the source. Introduce people and components unambiguously, then name them consistently. Apply requested anonymization consistently. Give fictional characters distinct names and keep the name-to-character mapping stable.

Pass: Each name identifies one referent, matches its source or fictional assignment, and follows the chosen naming convention.

### 16. Seek original images.

Target: A metaphor or comparison.

Requirement: Replace a stock metaphor with a concrete observation or a new comparison derived from the subject. For each comparison, identify the exact shared property. Keep one coherent image instead of mixing incompatible comparisons.

Pass: Every image supplies a specific correspondence; no stock or mixed metaphor survives.

### 17. Riff on the creative language of others.

Target: Quotation, paraphrase, or allusion.

Requirement: When borrowing language, distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and allusion. Quote exactly and credit the source; restate paraphrases in original wording and attribute distinctive borrowed ideas. Build the new passage around the present subject rather than copying another writer's expression.

Pass: Borrowed language has a clear source and form; new language does new work rather than masquerading as an original quotation.

### 20. Choose the number of elements with a purpose in mind.

Target: A series or list.

Requirement: Build series by function: one for focus, two for contrast, three for a completed pattern, four or more for inventory or accumulation. Give every item a distinct role. Retain every item the user requires; remove filler added only to reach a count.

Pass: The series count corresponds to its function; no duplicate or invented item exists solely to make a pattern.

### 21. Know when to back off and when to show off.

Target: Harm, grief, danger, major results, or flourishes.

Requirement: Describe harm, grief, danger, and major factual results with concrete nouns and direct verbs. Remove evaluative intensifiers and decorative commentary from those passages. In explanatory or playful passages, make each flourish carry a comparison, distinction, or insight.

Pass: High-stakes passages let the facts carry the weight; every flourish elsewhere adds identifiable meaning.

### 22. Climb up and down the ladder of abstraction.

Target: A central abstract claim.

Requirement: Pair each central abstract claim with a concrete example, observation, or measurement. Explain what the example establishes and where that inference stops. Connect concrete detail back to the general point instead of leaving either level unsupported.

Pass: The reader can move from claim to example and back; the inference does not exceed the evidence.

### 28. Put odd and interesting things next to each other.

Target: A contrast or comparison.

Requirement: Place the two sides of a contrast in adjacent sentences or paragraphs. Identify the shared comparison axis and state the revealing difference. Preserve mismatched baselines and uncertainty explicitly instead of implying equivalence.

Pass: The contrasting facts are adjacent, comparable on a named basis, and not presented as an unsupported causal relationship.

### 41. Turn procrastination into rehearsal.

Target: A blocked opening or argument.

Requirement: When blocked, rehearse one opening or speak through one version of the argument in a working note. Then write a provisional draft from it. Move unresolved details into marked gaps instead of restarting preparation.

Pass: A blocked start produces draft text after rehearsal rather than another planning loop.

### 42. Do your homework well in advance.

Target: New factual material or added claims.

Requirement: Before writing new factual material, identify and obtain the evidence for its key claims. Record source locations and unresolved gaps. For a supplied-text rewrite, use the supplied facts; verify any added claim before including it. Finish evidence collection before polishing factual claims.

Pass: Every added factual claim has a source; every unresolved dependency is marked rather than fabricated.

### 43. Read for both form and content.

Target: A model text.

Requirement: When using a model text, extract its argument and separately map its opening, evidence order, transitions, cadence, and ending. Reuse the structural technique in new wording. Keep claims from the model separate from verified facts for the current piece.

Pass: Content and form have separate working notes; borrowed structure does not become copied prose or unsourced evidence.

### 44. Save string.

Target: Reusable facts or context surfacing during the work.

Requirement: Capture reusable facts, observations, phrases, and questions in an authorized working note with provenance and date. Separate verified material from leads. Persist reusable context in the approved destination; retain task-only scraps in working notes.

Pass: Saved material has provenance, a date, and a fact-versus-lead label; nothing is persisted to an unauthorized destination.

### 48. Limit self-criticism in early drafts.

Target: Drafting.

Requirement: Complete a beginning-to-ending draft before line editing. Mark uncertainty in working notes and continue. After the complete draft exists, run the structural and sentence-level passes. Keep generating and judging as separate phases.

Pass: A complete draft precedes polishing; all temporary factual gaps are resolved or disclosed before delivery.

### 49. Learn from your critics.

Target: Supplied critique or reviewer feedback.

Requirement: For each critique received, identify the specific reader problem and the passage causing it. Correct factual errors. Apply editorial changes that fix the identified problem while preserving the brief. Account for a rejected recommendation with a concrete conflict or evidence-based reason.

Pass: Every criticism is addressed by a correction or a stated reason; none is ignored or accepted without examining the passage.

### 50. Own the tools of your craft.

Target: An audit.

Requirement: Treat every applied rule as the writing standard. Identify violations by rule number, repair them, and recheck the result. Keep authored rule wording in the branch files. Select rules through the branch tags and targets, never by dropping a rule that is difficult to satisfy.

Pass: The final check accounts for every applied rule, and for all 55 in an exhaustive audit. Every departure within that scope cites an explicit user override rather than editorial preference.

### 51. Take advantage of narrative numbers.

Target: Quantities.

Requirement: Attach units and timeframes to quantities. Give changes their baseline and comparison period. Distinguish counts, rates, percent changes, and percentage-point changes. Label estimates, extrapolations, and realized results. Verify each derived calculation and pair consequential figures with a concrete comparison from the evidence.

Pass: Every number has interpretable scope; comparisons and arithmetic are correct; projections remain labeled as projections.

### 52. Express your best thought in the shortest sentence.

Target: A central insight in a multi-sentence piece.

Requirement: State the central insight in a standalone sentence. Make it the shortest complete authored sentence in the piece; exact source quotations are outside that comparison. Move supporting explanation around it while preserving any qualification that determines its truth. Apply Rule 2 to its word order.

Pass: The central insight occupies the shortest complete authored sentence and remains accurate on its own.
